# Baseball2Vector: Testing Whether Interpretable Player Vectors Add Predictive Information

Scalar baseball metrics cannot distinguish players who produce similar value through different skill profiles. Baseball2Vector (B2V) compresses public season statistics into five interpretable dimensions—Contact, Power, Plate Discipline, Defense, and Speed. We test whether this structure adds next-season information beyond scalar baselines, constituent statistics, Marcel projections, and alternative encoders. All preprocessing uses input-season cohorts or training-only transforms, and all outer evaluations use rolling-origin splits.

Across five wRC+ origins from 2020–2025 (1,706 pooled test cases), B2V improved mean absolute error over a calibrated scalar baseline by 0.981 points (95% player-cluster bootstrap CI [0.619, 1.343]) and did so in all five origins. However, the 22 constituent statistics outperformed B2V by 0.180 points [0.008, 0.350], showing that the five-dimensional compression is nearly, but not fully, lossless. Adding B2V to a calibrated Marcel projection improved pooled MAE by 0.404 points [0.193, 0.608]. In the three-origin archived window, Z-score, PCA, and JointVAE encoders all beat the scalar baseline, suggesting that the shared grouping carries more of the signal than any single encoder. Comparable-player retrieval and WAR targets show that performance depends on the task and outcome definition.

These results support B2V as a compact, interpretable profile with modest incremental predictive value, not as a replacement for richer inputs or professional scouting grades.
